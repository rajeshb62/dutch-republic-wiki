Bor — Oorsprongk, Begin, en Vervolgh der Nederlandsche Oorlogen, Stuk I (Books 1–12)

Pieter Bor Christiaensz. (Pieter Christiaanszoon Bor, 1559–1635), Oorsprongk, Begin, en Vervolgh der Nederlandsche Oorlogen, beroerten, en Borgerlyke Oneenigheden; half-title De Nederlandsche Historiën. Beginning with Charles V’s grant of the Netherlands to Philip II and (for the whole work) running to the end of MDC (1600); “waerachtelijk en onpartijdelijk” narrated; divided into XXXVII Boeken and IV Stukken. This file is Het Eerste Stuk — Books 1–12. Amsterdam: Weduwe van Joannes van Someren, Abraham Wolfgangh, Hendrick en Dirck Boom, 1679, with privilege. Internet Archive identifier oorsprongkbegine01borp.

Bor is the great contemporary-style chronicler of the Revolt: dense year-by-year narrative stuffed with placards, letters, and public acts. Later stukken continue toward 1600 (the 1679 publishers also speak of a continuation toward 1620). Stuk I stops at the death of Don John of Austria (1578), with Bor promising next books will treat Parma’s government. Pair with hooft-nederlandsche-historien (literary Tacitean narrative to Leicester) and Motley (English digest); Bor is the documentary spine.

Completeness

Complete Eerste Stuk of the 1679 Someren/Wolfgangh/Boom edition: privilege, dedicatory poems, Books 1–12 (1555–1578), author’s short byvoegsel, publishers’ Byvoegsel van Authentyke Stukken (full texts of documents Bor only summarized), and register of that appendix. Not Stukken II–IV (Books 13–37). Whole-work title claims coverage to 1600 across four stukken.

Contents

Front matter

States of Holland privilege for reprinting Bor’s Nederlandsche Historie with plates and continuation. Half-title and full title; engravers’ and poets’ commendations (including Latin epigrams comparing Bor to Livy). Summarie of Book 1: geography and character of the Netherlands; Charles’s abdication; growth of Reformed religion; placards and Inquisition instructions; war with France; Granvelle; new bishoprics; Orange’s judgment on Trent/Inquisition policy.

Books 1–4 — Abdication to early Alva years (~1555–1568)

Eerste–Vierde Boek open on the provinces and Charles V’s 1555 transfer to Philip; Inquisition placards; Granvelle and the new bishoprics; Margaret of Parma; Compromise, Request, field preaching, iconoclasm; Alva’s arrival; Egmont and Horn; early Blood Council sentences. Documentary density is high (oaths, placards, names of inquisitors). Feeds philip-ii-of-spain, william-of-orange, margaret-of-parma, cardinal-granvelle, count-egmont, duke-of-alva, council-of-troubles, religion-and-politics.

Books 5–8 — War years through mid-1570s (~1569–1575)

Vijfde–Achtste Boek (running years into 1569, 1572, 1575) cover Alva’s fiscal and military regime, Sea Beggar rising, Haarlem, Alkmaar, and the Leiden war years — Bor’s citizen-soldier chronicle with muster lists and municipal detail (his own Delft/Haarlem relief notes appear later in the author’s byvoegsel). Feeds sea-beggars, siege-of-leiden, haarlem, leiden.

Books 9–12 — Pacification to Don John’s death (~1576–1578)

Negende–Twaelfde Boek (1576–1578) narrate Spanish Fury and Pacification of Ghent, Don John’s arrival and breach, Archduke Matthias, English diplomacy, Gent seizures, Amsterdam alarms, Groningen Landdag crisis, Don John declared enemy, Rennenberg at Deventer, and Don John’s illness and death — closing with a year-poem and Comines’ maxim on proud princes. Bor explicitly defers Parma’s regime to following books. Feeds pacification-of-ghent, don-john-of-austria, spanish-fury-antwerp, alexander-farnese-parma (threshold only), amsterdam, union-of-utrecht (approaching context).

Byvoegsels and register

Author’s short byvoegsel (e.g. names of Gouda citizens killed relieving Haarlem). Publishers’ May 1679 note: Bor’s fame rests on impartiality and authentic pieces; where he gave only titles or summaries, they print full texts (some never before printed) behind each stuk. Appendix register lists oaths of 1555, Orange’s 1568 Verantwoording, Hoogstraten and Horn defences, banishment placards, Orange’s 1572 Vermaning, Mechelen tyranny narrative, Requesens remonstrance (1574), Generality pieces against Don John (1577), etc.

Coverage for this wiki

For the Dutch Republic wiki, Bor Stuk I is especially useful for:

  • Documentary Revolt narrative 1555–1578 — placards, oaths, letters, city resolutions alongside story.
  • william-of-orange and early States politics before Leicester/Parma climax.
  • Alva / Blood Council / Sea Beggars / Leiden — narrative plus lists and actes.
  • Don John crisis — fullest Dutch chronicle close for Stuk I’s endpoint.
  • Contrast with Hooft — Bor = chronicle + pièces; Hooft = classical prose art to Leicester’s end.
